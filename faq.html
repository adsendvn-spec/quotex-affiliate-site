Legality & Safety

There is no simple "yes". Quotex is an offshore platform and is not authorised by SEBI or the RBI. Under FEMA rules, Indian residents may trade forex only through authorised entities on recognised exchanges, and the RBI has placed several online trading platforms, including Quotex, on its Alert List of unauthorised entities. Using such platforms therefore carries legal uncertainty and no Indian investor protection. Consult a qualified legal professional if you are unsure.

Technically, the platform uses SSL encryption, offers two-factor authentication and, in our testing, processed withdrawals reliably. However, it is not regulated by any top-tier authority, so there is no external body guaranteeing client funds or resolving disputes. Treat any deposit as money you can afford to lose.

Based on our own testing and reader reports, Quotex operates as a functioning trading platform that pays out withdrawals to verified users. The bigger risk is the product itself: short-term fixed-time options are high-risk, and many traders lose money. Beware of fake "Quotex" apps, Telegram account managers and signal sellers — those are common scams.

Generally, income from trading is taxable in India and should be declared in your income tax return. Foreign remittances may also attract TCS under the Liberalised Remittance Scheme. Tax treatment can be complex, so keep full records and speak to a chartered accountant.
